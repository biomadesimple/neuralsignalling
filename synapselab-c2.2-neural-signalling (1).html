Channel 02

How an impulse travels along a neuron

An action potential doesn't move like a wave in water — it is regenerated along the axon membrane. In a myelinated axon it can skip straight between nodes of Ranvier (saltatory conduction); in an unmyelinated axon it must regenerate at every point along the membrane. Press play and compare.

Speed

Myelin (an insulating, lipid-rich sheath) prevents ion leakage across the membrane, so the depolarization only needs to "jump" — regenerate — at the exposed nodes of Ranvier. This makes conduction much faster than in an unmyelinated axon, where every patch of membrane must depolarize in turn.

Channel 03

The action potential

A neuron only fires if a stimulus depolarizes the membrane past threshold (around −55 mV). Below threshold, nothing propagates. At or above it, a full, identical action potential fires every time — the all-or-nothing principle. Set a stimulus strength, fire it, then step through each phase at your own pace.
